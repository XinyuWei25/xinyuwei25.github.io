About

I am a PhD candidate in economics at ECARES, Université libre de Bruxelles, supervised by Bram De Rock. I study how households absorb income, health and care shocks, and how well social protection and standard welfare measures capture what they go through. My work uses large household panels from China, Europe, the UK and the US.

Before the PhD I worked on macro-finance. My MPhil thesis at the Chinese University of Hong Kong studied monetary policy and the labour share, and at the Shenzhen Finance Institute I wrote public reports on the US economy and taught applied econometrics.

I am on the 2026–27 job market and expect to complete my PhD in 2027.

Research

When Care Supply Becomes Care Demand: Functional Decline and Support Reallocation within Older Couples

Working paper, 2026
SHARECHARLS

When the spouse who provides care develops functional limitations, the original recipient becomes 9.7 percentage points less likely to receive spousal care, and outside help only partly fills the gap.

Abstract

Spouses provide much of the care that older adults receive, but caregivers age too. This paper follows older couples across eight waves of SHARE around the onset of functional limitations in the caregiving spouse. The original recipient becomes 9.7 percentage points less likely to receive spousal care, while additional outside support concentrates on the newly impaired caregiver, so lost care is only partly replaced. Household-level measures of service use can therefore hide a care gap for the person who was already dependent. A comparison with CHARLS provides evidence from China.

Income Risk Exposure and Consumption Smoothing in China

Submitted to China Economic Review
CFPS 2012–2022

Low-income households are more vulnerable mainly because they face larger income shocks, not because they smooth consumption less well.

Abstract

Using six waves of the China Family Panel Studies (2012–2022) and a BPP income-process model estimated by two-step GMM with bootstrap inference, I separate exposure to income shocks from their pass-through to consumption. Transitory income-shock variance is about 40% higher in the bottom than in the top income tercile, while consumption pass-through shows no systematic income gradient. The vulnerability of low-income households comes mainly from greater shock exposure rather than weaker smoothing.

Presented at the Mannheim Applied Seminar (March 2026) and the ECARES Internal Seminar (February 2026).

Time Poverty as a Constraint

Work in progress
LISSUKHLSPSID

Long hours are mostly chosen and carry little welfare cost. The cost comes from working more hours than one wants, worth about 12–23% of income, and standard measures miss most of the people affected.

Abstract

Time-adjusted poverty measures are increasingly used to argue for childcare, leave and working-time policies, but they are rarely validated against welfare. Using household panels from the Netherlands (LISS), the UK (Understanding Society) and the US (PSID), I show that hours-based time poverty mostly reflects chosen allocations of time. The hidden time poor are no less satisfied than others, and time-poor households do not suffer larger well-being losses after births, the onset of caregiving, partner health shocks or the COVID-19 school closures. The welfare-relevant object is a binding hours constraint: working more hours than one wants. It costs about 12–23% of income in equivalent terms, in line with estimates from administrative data, yet standard time-poverty and income measures miss 67–85% of those affected, who are concentrated in the upper half of the income distribution. Legal rights to request flexible or reduced hours do not relax the constraint; changing employer does. Identification relies on person fixed-effects event studies, with double/debiased machine learning as a robustness check.

Temporary Unemployment and Household Outcomes in Belgium

Work in progressJoint work, HAIOPOLICY project
EU-SILCBE-SILC

Workers placed on temporary unemployment in 2020 lost employment months, but their households showed no detectable income or poverty gap through 2022.

Abstract

During the COVID-19 crisis Belgium protected jobs through its temporary-unemployment scheme. Using EU-SILC/BE-SILC panels in an event-study design with a weighted comparison group, we find that exposed workers had 0.48 fewer employment months in 2020 but no detectable household-income or poverty gap through 2022. Labour-market recovery and household protection followed different paths.
