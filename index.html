Xinyu Wei

PhD candidate in Economics and Management

I study how households respond to income, employment and care shocks, and how social protection shapes their outcomes.

I am a doctoral researcher at ECARES, Université libre de Bruxelles, supervised by Bram De Rock. I expect to complete my PhD in 2027. My research combines household panel data with applied econometric methods to examine income risk, consumption smoothing, ageing and welfare.

Before my PhD, I worked on macroeconomic and financial-market reports at the Shenzhen Finance Institute and on historical data at Harvard’s China Gazetteer Project.

Research

Household risk and welfare

Working paper · 2026

When Care Supply Becomes Care Demand: Functional Decline and Support Reallocation within Older Couples

Using eight waves of SHARE, I examine care reallocation following the onset of functional limitations in a caregiving spouse. The original recipient becomes 9.7 percentage points less likely to receive spousal care, while additional outside support concentrates on the newly impaired caregiver. Household-level service growth can therefore mask incomplete replacement of lost care. The analysis includes comparative evidence from CHARLS in China.

Submitted to China Economic Review

Income Risk Exposure and Consumption Smoothing in China

Using six CFPS waves (2012–2022) and a BPP income-process model estimated by two-step GMM, I distinguish income-shock exposure from consumption pass-through. Transitory income-shock variance is approximately 40% higher in the bottom than the top income tercile, while consumption pass-through shows no systematic income gradient.

Presented at the Mannheim Applied Seminar, March 2026, and the ECARES Internal Seminar, February 2026.

Work in progress

Time Poverty as a Constraint

Using person fixed-effects event studies and double/debiased machine learning with LISS, UKHLS and PSID household panels, I examine whether time-poverty measures capture binding constraints or time-use choices. Current estimates associate working more hours than desired with welfare losses equivalent to approximately 12–23% of income; standard measures miss 67–85% of those affected.

Work in progress · Joint work under the HAIOPOLICY project

Temporary Unemployment and Household Outcomes in Belgium

We use an event-study design with EU-SILC/BE-SILC panels and a weighted comparison group to examine workers exposed to Belgium’s temporary-unemployment response in 2020. These workers had 0.48 fewer months of employment in 2020, with no statistically detectable household-income or poverty gap through 2022.
