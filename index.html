About

I am a PhD candidate in economics at ECARES, Université libre de Bruxelles, supervised by Bram De Rock. I expect to complete my PhD in 2027 and am on the 2026–27 job market.

My research focuses on household risk, labour economics, ageing and social protection. Using household panel data, I study how families respond to income, employment and care shocks, and how these responses affect their welfare.

Before joining ECARES, I studied at the Chinese University of Hong Kong and Georgetown University and worked at the Shenzhen Finance Institute, where I wrote reports on US macroeconomic developments and taught applied econometrics.

Research

When Care Supply Becomes Care Demand: Partner-Care Exit and the Destination of Outside Support

Working paper, 2026
SHARE

Caregivers’ functional decline changes both the supply of care and the need for help within older couples. Outside support expands, but some of the new assistance reaches the caregiver, so household support growth cannot be counted entirely as replacement for lost partner care.

Abstract

What happens when the partner providing care begins to need help? Using the Survey of Health, Ageing and Retirement in Europe (SHARE), this paper identifies existing personal-care relationships and follows both partners as the caregiver develops functional limitations. Overlap weighting and regression adjustment account for differences in baseline characteristics. Multiple new limitations are associated with a 20 percentage point higher probability of partner-care exit. Outside support is also more common, particularly through the joint use of help from children and home services. Following each partner separately shows that new assistance reaches the original caregiver as well as the original recipient. Household support expansion therefore combines help for the caregiver’s new needs with assistance to the partner who was already receiving care. These findings highlight the importance of assessing both members of a care relationship when planning support for older people living at home.

Income Risk Exposure and Consumption Smoothing in China

Working paper, 2026
CFPS 2012–2022

Low-income households are more vulnerable mainly because they face larger income shocks, not because they smooth consumption less well.

Abstract

Using six waves of the China Family Panel Studies (2012–2022) and a BPP income-process model estimated by two-step GMM with bootstrap inference, I separate exposure to income shocks from their pass-through to consumption. Transitory income-shock variance is about 40% higher in the bottom than in the top income tercile, while consumption pass-through shows no systematic income gradient. The vulnerability of low-income households comes mainly from greater shock exposure rather than weaker smoothing.

Presented at the Mannheim Applied Seminar (March 2026) and the ECARES Internal Seminar (February 2026).
